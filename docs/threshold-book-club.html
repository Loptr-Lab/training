THE THRESHOLD / READING-CIRCLE HANDBOOK

The Threshold reading circle: rollout and handoff

Return to the reading list

Owner direction: Dominique, November 10, 2026. This handbook establishes the self-paced reading practice and its contribution route. It does not schedule meetings, appoint facilitators, create membership or change story/game canon.

Start here

  1. Open https://loptr-lab.github.io/training/threshold.html. Training home also links to The Threshold · read 15 minutes a day. Holdings discovery and its desktop Projects window link to this same reader, rather than Mayhem.
  2. Choose one accessible book. Ask your local public library about print, ebook, audiobook and interlibrary loans. Search Open Library; a catalogue result is not a promise of a loan. Check resale bookshops, library sales and book exchanges before buying new. Independent bookshops can help; Amazon is an optional last resort. No purchase is required to participate.
  3. Read for 15 minutes a day, at your own pace. The timer is optional. Reading takes the fifteen minutes; reflection afterward is optional, not a quota.
  4. Write a question or a few thoughts in your own words if useful. Name the book and use your edition's chapter/page/audio locator. Different editions and reading speeds are welcome. Missing a day never locks anyone out.
  5. Keep notes private or choose an excerpt to discuss. The existing community entrance is https://made-sick.org/join.html. Introduce your book and one question there using its normal flow. No notes, identity or consent flags are carried in the link. This reader has no group feed or submission API.
  6. Explore https://loptr-lab.github.io/training/pixie-contributors.html when ready for a bounded contribution. Reading alone is a valid place to begin.

Comprehension practice, without grading people

Choose one or two questions; do not turn the whole list into homework.

PracticeSample questionA useful response contains
RecallWhat happened, or what claim did the author make?Two sentences in the reader's own words.
EvidenceWhich action, detail or example supports that interpretation?An edition locator and a distinction between observation and inference.
ClarificationWhat word, motive or reasoning step is unclear?A question and the surrounding context; uncertainty is acceptable.
PerspectiveWhose viewpoint is available? What other explanation could fit?An alternative reading and what evidence could change the reader's mind.
ApplicationWhich project thread does this illuminate? Where does the comparison fail?A specific connection with limits, rather than declaring two stories identical.
ReflectionDid writing or another reader's question change your understanding?A revision or an explanation of why the initial reading still fits.

Example scaffold: “I think ___ because the detail at ___ shows ___. Could ___ also explain it?” For craft writing: “The author claims ___. Would it still hold in this playtest situation?” These are scaffolds, not model answers. Accept a spoken contribution, private audio note or sketch as alternatives. Do not diagnose a reader, demand personal disclosures, or require programming or advanced mathematics to enter the circle.

Facilitation format another person can pick up

Self-paced participation is available without a host. A volunteer who wants to host a discussion must first agree a time, channel, accessibility arrangements and spoiler boundary with participants through existing community channels. No standing meeting or named host is announced by this release.

For a small optional discussion, use this adaptable 25-minute format:

  1. 3 minutes: name the book/edition and spoiler boundary; offer the right to pass.
  2. 7 minutes: invite one recall or clarification question, then locate evidence.
  3. 7 minutes: compare interpretations without scoring or forcing agreement.
  4. 5 minutes: choose one possible story, art or design connection; state its limits.
  5. 3 minutes: ask whether anyone wants a bounded original task. Record unresolved questions and consent to share a summary. Passing is a complete response.

Readers need not be at the same chapter. Separate groups or use passage-level spoiler labels when positions differ. Avoid unsolicited plot reveals, long copyrighted extracts, book scans or copied comic panels. Refer to editions and locations; summarize in your own words. A facilitator does not accept canon.

Notes and privacy

The optional timer and notebook operate in the browser, without accounts, network requests, analytics, publication, membership or cross-site transfer. The notebook keeps each book's notes separate. Saving to browser storage is unchecked by default and requires an explicit choice. This is local storage, not encryption or an account backup; avoid private information on shared devices. Download notes before clearing browser data or changing devices. Clearing the notebook cannot remove downloaded files. Share a chosen copy yourself through existing channels. There is no automatic submission or facilitator access.
