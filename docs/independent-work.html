Build your independent-work pathway

Use development and creative-technology skills for independent musicians, artists, creators and small businesses in underserved fields. Choose a next step that fits your skills, access needs and available time.

Training home Contributor checkpoints A. Contribute to a real project B. Find and deliver paid client work C. Build a sustainable freelance business

Choose one next action

These are three routes you can combine over time, not a traditional employer-placement pipeline. Start with your work → barriers → requirements → funding research → verified rules → request, using the existing planning journey.

  • Need a real contribution? Choose route A and ask a maintainer about one small task before beginning.
  • Have a service someone needs? Choose route B and prepare a small paid proposal.
  • Need to test business viability or support? Choose route C and prepare questions for an appropriate adviser.

No account is needed to read this guide. Use your own notes, the existing project checkpoint or a private client record. To request learning help, use the existing Training improvement process with only a public-safe question, chosen route, proposed scope and functional access needs. A request does not guarantee supervision.

Follow the existing mission and participation terms and collaboration and agreement guidance. Current Loptr Lab open contributions are voluntary and unpaid. Paid commissions require a separate signed agreement before work begins.

Route A · Volunteer contribution unless separately agreed

Contribute to a real project

Start with Mirlo for music-platform work. MetaBrainz / Picard and Funkwhale are additional research options. Check the source notes below; a project’s existence does not establish available tasks.

  1. Find a small need. Read the project’s contribution instructions and current issues; check assignments or propose a bounded improvement. For example, document one confusing setup step or reproduce one keyboard defect.
  2. Contact maintainers first. Use their published process, describe the issue and your proposed change, and ask whether it is useful before implementing. Mirlo explicitly invites discussion first. Picard asks coders to discuss work with its maintainers during v3 changes.
  3. Agree the working arrangement. Record scope, time limit, supervision/contact, accessibility needs, review expectations, attribution and exit terms. If nobody can review or support the task, reduce the scope or choose another task.
  4. Deliver one bounded contribution. Follow the project’s tests, licenses and submission process. Keep progress in its existing issue or review record, not a parallel Loptr Lab tracker.
  5. Keep evidence. Record exactly what you personally did, sources checked, feedback, submission link and what was accepted. Use the existing portfolio evidence fields.

An open-source contribution is not automatically paid work, an internship, an accepted partnership or guaranteed mentoring. A submitted change may be declined. Acceptance, merge and deployment are separate events; none guarantees employment.

Route B · Paid client work

Find and deliver paid client work

Begin with your own relationships with independent artists, musicians and small creative businesses. Ask about a task that is actually blocking their work: an accessible event page, a simple catalog update, or a documented media workflow. Contra is one possible client-discovery channel alongside those relationships.

Platform access, fees, eligibility, payment support and demand vary. Read current terms yourself; no channel promises clients or income. You do not need to buy access to continue this free training.

  1. Define the service and need. State who needs what result and why. Confirm success criteria, existing tools, timing and the client’s actual budget; do not assume they need a new website or AI system.
  2. Write a small paid proposal. Include the need, included/excluded work, deliverables, schedule, review contact and proposed price. Example: “Improve keyboard navigation on your existing event page; deliver the patch, test notes and handoff. Price: __; payment milestones: __; revisions: __; maintenance: __.” Fill in and agree every blank.
  3. Agree commercial details before starting. Use the existing agreement guidance for deliverables, price, payment milestones, revision limits, maintenance/support, cancellation and safe exit. Paid work needs a separate signed agreement. Do not require unpaid trial work.
  4. Confirm access and rights. Record accessibility requirements and how they will be checked. Confirm ownership, licenses, third-party permissions, credits and who can approve publication. Use 50 Ways’ rights and agreement learning context to prepare questions for an appropriate professional, not as substitute legal terms.
  5. Review, deliver and document. Obtain review against the agreed criteria. Deliver the artifact, accessible documentation, maintenance boundaries and handover instructions. Confirm milestone acceptance and payment through the agreed private process.
  6. Ask before showing the work. Obtain explicit permission for portfolio use, credit and redactions. Preserve your own contribution and review status in the training-plan evidence fields; keep contracts, prices and correspondence private unless disclosure is agreed.

Route C · Business support and individual planning

Build a sustainable freelance business

In the United States, prioritize the NDI Small Business Hub, a local Small Business Development Center and your applicable state vocational rehabilitation program. Outside the US, use the existing jurisdiction-based funding research to find the responsible local authority.

  1. Prepare viability evidence. Describe one service, likely customers, a real need, alternatives, evidence from customer conversations and what remains a hypothesis. Do not treat interest as a paying customer.
  2. Estimate delivery and startup costs. Record time, pricing assumptions, payment delays, software, equipment, accessibility tools, maintenance and ongoing expenses. Compare minimum, preferred and alternative setups using the technology plan and quote comparison.
  3. Prepare adviser questions. Ask how to test demand, develop customers, evaluate viability and identify accessible support. Ask what evidence and plan are needed, what costs might qualify and which decisions need written preapproval. Record source, date, decision-maker and unanswered questions using the source-verification record.
  4. Check your applicable state program. Ask about individual eligibility, an appropriate employment plan, self-employment approval, equipment, startup costs and follow-up requirements. Keep personal benefits, financial and medical evidence in the agency’s designated private channel.
  5. Resolve individual financial questions. Reuse the benefits-safe founder path and ask qualified advisers about tax, benefits and housing reporting before relying on an assumption.

Minnesota VRS is a state example, not a nationwide entitlement. The small-business policy needs a current recheck. Funding requires individual approval and an appropriate plan. There is no promise of payment for Loptr Lab training, equipment or ongoing business expenses. Follow the existing Minnesota participant path and obtain written authorization before relying on support.

Keep reusable evidence of your work

Adapt the existing training plan and portfolio evidence fields, then keep the record beside the work. Include the need and agreed scope, your specific contribution, accessibility requirements and checks, tools and AI assistance, sources and verification, review feedback, display permission/redactions, the artifact and unfinished work.

Mark proposed → submitted → accepted → deployed separately, with a date and evidence for each applicable status. A proposal is not accepted work; a merge is not proof of deployment. Record failed checks and unverified outcomes. Publication does not guarantee employment.

AI supplies research to verify, not authority to obey. Check original sources and retain human decisions. Share only a permitted summary in the existing contributor checkpoint. Keep client records and private learner information out of public GitHub.
