## Resource inventory and exact mappings

### The Animation Workshop

- Publisher material: [Student-work showcase](https://animationworkshop.via.dk/).
- Evidence/status: **verified-page**. VIA's official page names student productions including The Saga of Biorn and describes its animation education. This is a showcase, not a step-by-step tutorial.
- Access: External showcase; select accessible material. Viewing and media accessibility were not independently tested.
- Suggested level: Beginner observation.
- Loptr Lab objective: Explain a visual storytelling choice before planning an original shot.
- Reference family: **visual**; exact mapped categories: Interpretation; Evaluation.
- Practice: Choose one work from the publisher's showcase. Record its title and creator credits, then describe a staging or composition choice with a specific visual reference. Draw three original storyboard panels communicating a different action.

### School of Motion

- Publisher material: [Animation Principle: Master Natural Movements with Follow Through](https://schoolofmotion.com/blog/follow-through-tutorial).
- Evidence/status: **verified-page**. Publisher article explicitly teaches follow-through in After Effects and credits Jacob Richardson and Ryan Plummer.
- Access: Public article; After Effects is commercial software. Written explanation is available; video playback and captions not tested.
- Suggested level: Beginner to intermediate; basic After Effects familiarity.
- Loptr Lab objective: Demonstrate follow-through in a short original animation.
- Reference family: **animation**; exact mapped categories: Technical; Completeness and Quality; Creativity; Following Project Guidelines.
- Practice: Animate an original shape or character stopping while a secondary part continues moving. Keep a before-and-after comparison and explain one timing revision.

### Purple Pie Studios

- Publisher material: [Illustration lesson in the Explainer Animation Course for Beginners (descriptive label)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=w1C6UuwjtKc).
- Evidence/status: **user-confirmed-access**. Indexed YouTube publisher metadata identifies Purple Pie Studios and describes the second course video as creating illustrations for an explainer. Full playback could not be verified.
- Access: User confirmed this link works on 8 November 2026. Playback was not independently tested.
- Suggested level: Beginner; Loptr Lab placement.
- Loptr Lab objective: Prepare an original illustration for an explainer storyboard.
- Reference family: **animation**; exact mapped categories: Creativity; Following Project Guidelines.
- Practice: Create an original three-panel explainer and document which illustration elements would move independently.

### CG Cookie

- Publisher material: [BLENDER BASICS: An Introduction to Blender 3D 4.5 LTS](https://www.cgcookie.com/courses/blender-basics-an-introduction-to-blender-3d-4-5-lts).

- Access: Free publisher account required; no credit card advertised. Blender version guidance may change. Enrollment and full playback not tested.
- Suggested level: Beginner.
- Loptr Lab objective: Create and document a simple original Blender asset.
- Reference family: **visual**; exact mapped categories: Proficiency with media; Creativity aligns with intended communication..
- Practice: Use the navigation and modeling sections to make an original prop from basic shapes. Save the editable project and one still render. Explain how a modeling choice supports its intended use.

## Assessment references: what was actually verified

1. **“12 Principles of Animation Project Rubric”, Adobe Education Exchange host**
   https://cdn.edex.adobe.com/v3/uploads/resourceFile/2014/8543b976-6102-11e2-9a53-12313b016471.99d43c63-a0f9-4277-b632-5b37818d5e3c.pdf
   One-page PDF retrieved. Individual author is not identified in the PDF: do not
   assert Adobe authored it. Four original categories and 4–1 levels verified.
   Use the original descriptors by reference; no new weights or pass mark.
2. **University of Nebraska–Lincoln, ACE 2D**
   https://ace.unl.edu/ace-2-rubric/
   Retrieved page includes both Production and Interpretation, each with 4–1
   descriptors. Production concerns proficiency with media and creative
   communication; Interpretation concerns explaining and evaluating visual work.
   The mapped section, rather than the unrelated ACE 2A/B/C tables, must be used.
3. **University of Technology Sydney, “Video assessment criteria categories”**
   https://educationexpress.uts.edu.au/collections/using-video-for-assessment/resources/video-assessment-criteria-categories/
   Retrieved page provides adaptable categories and example evidence, expressly
   non-prescriptive. It is NOT a ready-made numbered VFX scoring rubric. Local
   reviews use qualitative evidence and feedback for selected categories.
   Accessibility and attribution/permissions are explicit source topics.
4. **University of Tampa, animation portfolio/capstone resources**
   https://www.ut.edu/academics/college-of-arts-and-letters/department-of-film-animation-and-new-media/bachelor-of-fine-arts-in-animation
   Official page retrieved; lists links to FMX 398 portfolio and FMX 499 capstone
   rubric PDFs. Linked as optional advanced reading only. No descriptors, scale
   or advanced grading is imported into the introductory activities.
